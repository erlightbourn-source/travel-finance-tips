Points vs. miles — what's the difference?

Loosely: "miles" usually means a specific airline's currency (Delta SkyMiles, for example), and "points" can mean either a hotel currency or a bank's flexible currency (like Chase or Amex points). The important split isn't the name — it's whether a currency is locked to one brand or transferable to many.

What are points actually worth?

A point isn't worth a fixed dollar amount — it's worth what you redeem it for. A rough way to think about it: value = the cash price of what you book ÷ the points it cost. If a flight costs $300 or 25,000 points, that redemption is about 1.2 cents per point. Under ~1 cent, you're usually better off paying cash and keeping the points. We frame value as a range, never a guarantee, because it swings with how you redeem — it's a method, not a promise.

Why beginners should start with transferable points

Transferable points (from cards like Chase Sapphire or Amex) can move to several airline and hotel partners. That flexibility is the whole point for a beginner: you're not betting your rewards on one airline's award chart. Single-airline miles can be great if you're loyal — but if plans change, flexible points give you more exits. This is why our starter path is flexible points first, co-brand miles later.

How you earn (without overthinking it)

  • Everyday spend on a rewards card you'd use anyway — autopay in full, never carry a balance for points (interest erases the reward).
  • Welcome offers — the fastest points, but only chase one you can hit with normal spending. Never manufacture spend you can't pay off. (Current offers change weekly — always confirm on the issuer's page.)
  • Bonus categories & portals (like Rakuten) that stack on top of card rewards.

How to redeem without getting burned

  1. Know the cash price first so you can judge if the points deal is good.
  2. Compare transfer partners before booking through a portal — sometimes transferring to an airline beats the portal price, sometimes it's worse.
  3. Don't hoard forever — points can devalue; use them within a reasonable horizon.
  4. Watch fees — award flights can carry taxes/surcharges; factor those in.

Five beginner mistakes

  1. Carrying a balance to earn points (interest > rewards, every time).
  2. Opening a premium fee card before the no-fee habit is solid.
  3. Locking into one airline's miles too early.
  4. Redeeming points for low-value stuff (gift cards, merchandise) instead of travel.
  5. Chasing a welcome offer you can't hit without overspending.
