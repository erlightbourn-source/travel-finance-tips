Quick answer

For most beginners, the best first credit card is a no-annual-fee card you can pay in full automatically — it builds credit for free while you learn the game. No credit history yet? How to choose (the honest version)

  1. No credit yet? A no-fee secured card first — soft-pull check, one at a time.
  2. Just want cash back? A flat 2% no-fee card + Rakuten + a high-yield savings account.
  3. Ready to travel? Transferable points before airline miles.
  4. Loyal to one brand? A co-brand can win — for your brand — do the math.
  5. Fee card? Only if the credits you'll actually use beat the fee — and price the value options (like Venture X) first.

Whatever you pick, apply on the issuer's official page, read the current terms there, and give it time.

FAQ

What's the best credit card for someone with no credit history?

Usually a no-annual-fee secured card you can autopay in full — you put down a refundable deposit, and it reports to the bureaus so your score builds. You're optimizing for on-time history, not rewards, in year one. Check for a soft-pull pre-qualification first so you don't waste a hard inquiry.

Do you only recommend cards you earn a commission on?

No — and that's the point of this page. We lead with the genuine best card in each category. Where that's a card we carry, the link is a referral link and we may earn at no cost to you. Where the best pick isn't one we hold (secured cards, flat-2% cash back, Venture X), we say so and link you straight to the issuer, earning nothing.

Should a beginner get a travel card or a cash-back card first?

Cash back if you're still building the pay-in-full habit; a transferable-points travel card once that's automatic and you actually travel. Transferable points (not single-airline miles) keep your options open.

Is an annual fee ever worth it for a beginner?

Only if the credits and perks you'll genuinely use add up to more than the fee — run that math for your real spending, not the headline. Price the best-value fee cards (like Venture X) before the priciest ones. Most beginners are better off no-fee for a while.

How many cards should I apply for at once?

One. Space applications out, let each account age, and never chase multiple bonuses before the fundamentals (autopay, low utilization) are solid.
